## Interview Loop: Moveworks — Senior Software Engineer

> Data compiled from **7+ sources** across Glassdoor, Blind, InterviewQuery, 1Point3Acres, and GeeksforGeeks. Most recent data: **2025**. Search date: May 11, 2026.
> ⚠️ **Note**: Moveworks was [acquired by ServiceNow in December 2025](https://careers.servicenow.com/jobs/744000113248682/senior-software-engineer-agentic-ai-product-moveworks/). The process below reflects pre/peri-acquisition data. Post-acquisition changes to the interview loop are not yet well-documented — confirm current structure with your recruiter.

### Overview

- **Rounds**: 5–8 total (2 phone screens + full virtual onsite loop)
- **Total duration**: 

The whole process typically takes around 4 weeks.

 However, 

candidates applying for Senior Software Engineer roles take an average of 13 days to get hired, according to Glassdoor data from 7 user-submitted interviews.


- **Format**: Fully virtual (phone screens + virtual onsite)
- **Difficulty**: 

Senior Software Engineer applicants have rated the interview process at Moveworks with 3.2 out of 5 for difficulty, and assessed their interview experience as 29% positive.


- **Recency of data**: 2021–2025

### Round-by-Round Breakdown

---

#### Round 1: Recruiter Screen
- **Duration**: ~30 minutes
- **Format**: Phone/video call
- **What they test**: Background, experience fit, culture alignment



The process begins with a call from a recruiter, which usually lasts about 30 minutes. During this conversation, the recruiter will discuss the role, the company culture, and the candidate's background.





Behavioral questions may also be part of the screening process. Sometimes, the recruiter might be perceived as condescending, but it is important to remain professional and focused. In certain cases, the hiring manager may join during the screening round to answer queries about the role and the company, and may also indulge in surface-level technical and behavioral discussions.



---

#### Round 2: Technical Phone Screen (Coding)
- **Duration**: ~60 minutes
- **Format**: Video call with live coding (Codility or similar platform)
- **What they test**: Data structures, algorithms, problem-solving



Following the initial call, candidates will participate in a technical phone interview. This round often includes algorithmic coding questions and may involve problem-solving exercises that test the candidate's understanding of data structures and algorithms. Candidates should be prepared for questions that require them to demonstrate their coding skills, often using platforms like LeetCode or similar.





One candidate reported a 1-hour technical interview with a senior engineer that included a problem similar to "word break."

 Another Blind user confirmed 

they received a hard-level question for the phone interview.

 The coding platform has been noted as Codility in some threads.

**Coding themes seen at this stage**: string manipulation, dynamic programming, recursion/backtracking (see Questions mode for specifics).

---

#### Round 3: Technical Phone Screen (System Design) *(for Senior+ roles)*
- **Duration**: ~60 minutes
- **Format**: Video call
- **What they test**: Architecture, scalability, distributed systems



For Senior SWE roles, candidates complete two phone screens — one coding and one system design — before the onsite loop.

 This is distinct from the typical SWE process (which may only have one phone screen) and appears to be a Senior-level gate.

---

#### Round 4: Virtual Onsite Loop
- **Duration**: Half-day to full day; 

the onsite loop typically includes 4–5 rounds and can total up to 6 hours.

 One Senior SWE candidate reported 

an 8-round onsite loop.


- **Format**: Back-to-back virtual video rounds
- **Breakdown of onsite sub-rounds**:

---

##### Onsite Sub-Round A: Coding Rounds (×2)
- **Duration**: ~60 minutes each
- **What they test**: Practical coding, edge cases, algorithmic thinking



The onsite interview is a more comprehensive evaluation, typically lasting several hours and consisting of multiple rounds. Candidates can expect to face two coding interviews and two system design interviews. The coding interviews will focus on practical coding challenges.





Coding rounds were described as medium-level LeetCode questions — not that difficult, but practice on LeetCode is key to crack them.

 

Questions are medium-hard LeetCode style, similar to string and hashing problems.



---

##### Onsite Sub-Round B: System Design Rounds (×2)
- **Duration**: ~60 minutes each
- **What they test**: Scalable system architecture; one round may be standard systems design, while the other leans more toward product/UX design



The system design rounds were described as medium level with a deep focus on explaining core concepts. Overall, the difficulty level was close to hard.



One candidate flagged a notable gotcha: 

they didn't realize the 2nd system design round was more like a product design round rather than a technical systems design round, and really struggled as a result.

 **Be prepared for both a classic distributed systems design question and a product/feature design question.**

---

##### Onsite Sub-Round C: Product Demo Round
- **Duration**: ~30 minutes
- **Format**: Moveworks employee demos the product to you
- **What they test**: Your ability to understand and engage with the product; cultural signal



There's a product demo round which is very helpful to get a really good understanding of the product you're going to be working on.

 

This round evaluates the candidate's fit within the team and their understanding of the product.



This is a **passive-to-interactive** round — come prepared with thoughtful questions about the product and how engineering decisions are made.

---

##### Onsite Sub-Round D: Hiring Manager (HM) Round
- **Duration**: ~45–60 minutes
- **Format**: Video call
- **What they test**: Behavioral fit, past experience, leadership signals, team alignment



The onsite also includes a hiring manager round.

 

In some cases, candidates may have a final interview with senior leadership or the hiring manager. This round is often more focused on behavioral questions and assessing the candidate's long-term vision and alignment with the company's goals. It may also include discussions about past experiences and how they relate to the challenges faced at Moveworks.



One candidate on Glassdoor had a notably negative HM experience: 

the first round was coding, and the second was the hiring manager, but the HM seemed "hell bent in dissuading" them that the role wasn't for them, taking responses out of context to justify rejection during the interview.

 This appears to be an outlier but worth being aware of.

---

##### Onsite Sub-Round E: Leadership/CTO Round *(Senior roles)*
- **Duration**: ~30–60 minutes
- **Format**: Video call



The virtual onsite has also included a chat with the CTO in some candidate reports.

 This round appears most common for senior/staff-level roles and is focused on vision, leadership, and long-term alignment.

### Red Flags & Insider Notes

- **High round count for Senior roles**: 

One Senior SWE candidate reported completing two phone screens followed by an 8-round onsite loop

 — this is at the high end. 

Multiple candidates described the interview process as "convoluted."



- **Backend-heavy even for frontend roles**: 

Despite frontend roles, the interviews are backend-heavy. Brush up on backend skills, system design, and algorithmic challenges to cover all bases.



- **Interviewer engagement varies**: 

One engineer hosting the technical interview was described as "extremely disengaged and gave zero positive or negative feedback/affirmations during the problem," which negatively affected the candidate's performance.



- **Down-leveling at offer stage**: Multiple Blind threads mention candidates being offered a lower level than expected after completing the full loop.

- **System design surprise**: The second system design round may be product design, not architecture — 

candidates have been caught off guard by this.



- **High engineering bar**: 

Internal reviews note a high engineering bar and talent overall, with a modern tech stack and good rigor in practices.



- **ServiceNow acquisition (Dec 2025)**: 

Moveworks was acquired by ServiceNow in December 2025, combining ServiceNow's workflow automation with Moveworks' Reasoning Engine and natural language capabilities.

 Process details may shift — confirm current loop structure with your recruiter.
