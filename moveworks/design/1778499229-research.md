## Interview Loop: Moveworks — Senior Software Engineer

### Overview
- **Rounds**: 5–8 total across all stages (recruiter screen → tech screen → HM screen → virtual onsite with 2 coding + 2 system design + 1 HR/behavioral + 1 product demo; occasionally a leadership "sell" chat with CEO/CTO)
- **Total duration**: 

Average of ~24 days across all roles

; 

some candidates reported the full process taking 4 weeks


- **Format**: Fully virtual (video + shared coding environment/codepad)
- **Difficulty rating**: 

Software Engineer applicants have rated the interview process at Moveworks with 3.3 out of 5 on difficulty, with 35% of candidates rating the experience as positive


- **Recency of data**: 2021–January 2025 (most detailed onsite breakdowns from 2024)

---

### Round-by-Round Breakdown

---

#### Round 1: Recruiter Phone Screen
- **Duration**: ~30 minutes
- **Format**: Phone/video call
- **What they test**: Background verification, role fit, culture alignment
- 

The process begins with a call from a recruiter, which usually lasts about 30 minutes. During this conversation, the recruiter will discuss the role, the company culture, and the candidate's background.


- 

Behavioral questions may also be part of the screening process.


- 

In certain cases, the hiring manager may join during the screening round to answer questions about the role and the company. They may also indulge in surface-level technical and behavioral discussions.


- ⚠️ **Candidate note**: 

Sometimes, the recruiter might be perceived as condescending — it is important to remain professional and focused.



---

#### Round 2: Technical Phone Screen
- **Duration**: ~45–60 minutes
- **Format**: Virtual coding (codepad-like environment)
- **What they test**: Data structures & algorithms, coding ability
- 

Following the initial call, candidates will participate in a technical phone interview. This round often includes algorithmic coding questions and may involve problem-solving exercises that test the candidate's understanding of data structures and algorithms. Candidates should be prepared for questions that require them to demonstrate their coding skills, often using platforms like LeetCode or similar.


- One LeetCode Discuss report (SDE-2/L4 level): 

Tech Screen was 45 minutes — a variant of a harder-side problem with follow-up questions (e.g., a problem similar to LeetCode "Synonymous Sentences").


- Confirmed question themes: word break variants, string manipulation, dynamic programming on strings

---

#### Round 3: Hiring Manager (HM) Screen
- **Duration**: ~30 minutes
- **Format**: Video call
- **What they test**: Project/experience deep dive, role fit, team alignment
- 

The HM Screen includes a project deep dive and technical questions.


- 

One candidate noted: "First round is coding. Second is hiring manager, but the HM seems hell bent in dissuading me this is not the role for me, taking my response out of context."

 *(flagged as an outlier experience)*

---

#### Rounds 4–8: Virtual Onsite Loop
- **Duration**: Several hours total (full day or split across two days)
- **Format**: Video-based virtual onsite with multiple back-to-back rounds

The most detailed firsthand breakdown of the onsite comes from a July 2024 Glassdoor report:



After the recruiter and tech screens, candidates are invited to a virtual onsite loop with 2 coding rounds, 2 system design rounds, 1 HR round, and a 30-minute product demo. The coding questions were medium-hard LeetCode style — similar to string and hashing problems. The system design rounds were also medium level with a deep focus on explaining core concepts. Overall, the level was close to hard.



Multiple other candidates corroborate a similar structure:



Virtual onsite consisting of two coding and two system design interviews along with product demo, chat with CTO and hiring manager as well.





One candidate described a 7–8 round process: first round on a coding platform, next 3 rounds all coding (easy, medium, and tough respectively), then 2 rounds of system design, 1 project round, and 1 HM round.



Here's the sub-breakdown of the onsite:

---

##### Onsite Sub-Round A: Coding Rounds (×2)
- **Duration**: ~60 minutes each
- **What they test**: DSA — strings, hashing, recursion, dynamic programming
- 

One Senior SWE candidate reported: "I had multiple coding rounds and system design rounds. The coding rounds were medium level LeetCode questions — not that difficult, but if you practice/prepare on LeetCode it's easy to crack."


- 

Expect basic LeetCode algorithmic coding questions and queries on backtracking, string manipulation, and recursion. Despite a role being frontend-focused, be prepared for backend-heavy queries and system design problems.


- Sample topics confirmed by candidates: substring sum, Jaccard score/string similarity, mathematical expression evaluation, hashing problems

---

##### Onsite Sub-Round B: System Design Rounds (×2)
- **Duration**: ~60 minutes each
- **What they test**: Standard distributed systems design + one round that is more "product design"
- 

Interview rounds mostly include DSA and system design. There's also a product demo round which is very helpful to get a really good understanding of the product you're going to be working on.


- ⚠️ **Gotcha flagged by a candidate**: 

One candidate was caught off guard: "I didn't have a single idea prior to the interview that the 2nd system design is a bit different and more like product design instead. So I really struggled."


- Sample system design topics mentioned: inverted index / search engine design, AWS billing system design

---

##### Onsite Sub-Round C: HR / Behavioral Round
- **Duration**: ~60 minutes
- **What they test**: Cultural fit, values alignment, past project experience
- 

In some cases, candidates may have a final interview with senior leadership or the hiring manager. This round is often more focused on behavioral questions and assessing the candidate's long-term vision and alignment with the company's goals.


- 

Moveworks places importance on cultural fit, so prepare for behavioral questions that assess your values and work ethic. Reflect on your past experiences and be ready to share specific examples. Use the STAR method to structure your responses effectively.


- Confirmed behavioral question: *"Tell me about the hardest task/project you solved."*

---

##### Onsite Sub-Round D: Product Demo (~30 min)
- **Duration**: ~30 minutes
- **Format**: A Moveworks team member demos the product to the candidate
- **What they test**: This is NOT a technical round — it's an informational session to help candidates understand what they'd be working on. It also serves as a chance for Moveworks to "sell" the role.
- 

There's a product demo round which is very helpful to get a really good understanding of the product you're going to be working on.



---

##### Optional: Leadership "Sell" Chats (CEO / CTO)
- Some candidates — particularly at more senior levels — reported informational conversations with executive leadership
- 

One candidate described: "1 preliminary technical screen, 1 phone screen with a hiring manager, onsite with systems and algo interviews, [and] informational 'sell' chats with CEO, CTO, and hiring manager."


- Another Blind user confirmed: *"I met with the CTO and I am really impressed at how the company is doing."*

### Red Flags & Insider Notes

1. **Process is long and can feel convoluted.** 

One candidate summarized it as: "Recruiter screen, technical interview, HM screen, more technicals then an onsite — extremely long process."



2. **Interviewers may be disengaged during coding rounds.** 

One candidate noted: "The engineer hosting my technical interview was extremely disengaged and gave zero positive or negative feedback during the problem. Would have had a much better experience had they given even a nod of acknowledgment."



3. **Even frontend roles are backend-heavy.** 

Despite it being a frontend role, the interviews are backend-heavy. Brush up on backend skills, system design, and algorithmic challenges to cover all bases.



4. **Down-leveling at offer stage is a known pattern.** Multiple Blind threads note candidates being offered a lower level than applied for — worth clarifying level expectations early with the recruiter.

5. **Post-acquisition context.** 

Moveworks was acquired by ServiceNow in December 2025. The combined entity is positioned to deliver an AI platform combining ServiceNow's workflow automation with Moveworks' Reasoning Engine and natural language capabilities.

 The interview process may evolve — confirm current structure with your recruiter.

6. **High engineering bar confirmed by employees.** 

A former employee noted: "High engineering bar and talent overall. Tech stack is very modern and up-to-date. Good rigor in practices."
