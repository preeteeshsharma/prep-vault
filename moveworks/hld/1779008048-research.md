## Recruiter-Confirmed Signal (Post Coding Round Call — May 2026)

> **Highest confidence signal.** After passing the coding rounds, the recruiter explicitly flagged these as the focus areas for the system design rounds. Treat these as near-certain evaluation axes.

### Confirmed Technology: Elasticsearch / OpenSearch
- Expect the search engine design to be framed at the Elasticsearch/OpenSearch level, not just a whiteboard inverted index
- Know the ES data model: indices → shards → segments (each segment is an immutable inverted index on disk)
- Know how ES routes documents to shards: `hash(doc_id) % num_primary_shards`
- Know replica shards vs primary shards and what each is responsible for

### Confirmed Evaluation Axis: Partitioning Design Depth
- This is explicitly what interviewers go deep on — don't just say "shard it"
- Know and be able to compare **alternate partitioning schemes**:

| Scheme | How it works | When to use | Gotcha |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Document-based (hash)** | `hash(docID) % N` routes each doc to a shard | Default ES approach; even distribution | A query must fan out to all shards |
| **Term-based** | Each shard owns a subset of the vocabulary | Queries only hit 1 shard | Hot shards (common terms like "the" get hammered) |
| **Range-based (time)** | Shard by document creation date / index rollover | Time-series logs, audit data | Hot shard problem on write (latest shard takes all writes) |
| **Geographic** | Shard by tenant region or data residency | Multi-region, compliance constraints | Cross-region queries expensive |

- For an enterprise search use case at Moveworks: document-based partitioning is the safe default; justify why and know when you'd deviate (e.g., multi-tenant isolation → tenant-based routing)

### Confirmed Evaluation Axis: API Completeness
- Interviewers evaluate whether your API covers **the full surface**, not just the happy path
- For a search engine, a complete API includes:
  - `POST /documents` — index a document
  - `PUT /documents/{id}` — update (reindex)
  - `DELETE /documents/{id}` — remove
  - `GET /search?q=...&filters=...&page=...` — query with pagination
  - `GET /documents/{id}` — fetch by ID
  - `POST /bulk` — batch indexing
  - `GET /health` / `GET /stats` — observability endpoints
  - Consider: versioning (`/v1/`), auth headers, content negotiation
- Omitting delete, bulk, or pagination signals incomplete thinking

## Interview Loop: Moveworks — Senior Software Engineer

### Overview

- **Rounds**: 5–6 total (2 phone screens + 3–6 onsite rounds depending on variation)
- **Total duration**: 

Candidates applying for Senior Software Engineer roles take an average of **13 days** to get hired (faster than Moveworks' overall average of 24 days), based on 7 submitted interviews.


- **Format**: Fully virtual (video + screen share); no in-person onsite reported
- **Difficulty rating**: 

Senior SWE applicants rated the interview difficulty **3.2 out of 5** and reported only **29% positive experience** — below Moveworks' company average of 40.8%.


- **Recency of data**: 2021–May 2025 (most usable recent data from 2024–2025)

> ⚠️ **Note on company status**: 

Moveworks was acquired by ServiceNow in December 2025.

 Interview process may evolve post-acquisition — treat the most recent data with this in mind.

---

### Round-by-Round Breakdown

---

#### Round 1: Recruiter Screen
- **Duration**: ~30 minutes
- **Format**: Phone/video call
- 

A recruiter from the Talent Acquisition Team contacts you to verify your background, experience, and skill level. Behavioral questions may be part of the screening. The whole call lasts about 30 minutes.


- 

In some cases, the hiring manager may join to answer questions about the role, and may touch on surface-level technical and behavioral topics.



---

#### Round 2: Technical Phone Screen (Coding)
- **Duration**: ~60 minutes
- **Format**: Virtual, with a coding platform (candidates report **Codility** as a common platform)
- 

After the recruiter round, you proceed to a technical screening round usually conducted via video conference and screen sharing. Questions revolve around coding challenges, data structures, and algorithms.


- **What's tested**: String manipulation and dynamic programming are heavily reported. 

Two challenging DSA rounds focused on string manipulation have been mentioned, with specific questions like the "Word Break 2" LeetCode problem.


- Recent phone screen theme: 

Multiple 2024–2025 candidates on 1point3acres reported a **word/hangman guessing game-style** coding challenge at the phone screen stage for SWE roles.



---

#### Round 3–8: Virtual Onsite Loop
- **Duration**: 

The onsite includes up to **8 rounds** (coding, system design, demo, hiring manager, and leadership), with over **6 hours** of onsite time alone.


- **Format**: All virtual; rounds are conducted on separate video calls, often on the same day or spread across 1–2 days

The most commonly reported onsite structure (corroborated across multiple sources):

---

#### Onsite — Coding Rounds (×2)
- **Duration**: ~60 minutes each
- 

Candidates report 2 coding rounds in the onsite loop with medium-hard LeetCode-style questions, similar to string and hashing problems. System design rounds are medium level with a deep focus on explaining core concepts. Overall difficulty is close to hard.


- 

Coding rounds are medium-level LeetCode questions — if you practice on LeetCode, they are manageable.


- Sample confirmed question types: string manipulation, dynamic programming on strings, hashmap/intersection problems, expression evaluation (postfix/BODMAS)

---

#### Onsite — System Design Rounds (×2)
- **Duration**: ~45–60 minutes each
- 

Candidates face two system design interviews. These focus on practical coding challenges and assess the ability to architect scalable systems.


- The system design rounds have a **"deep focus on explaining core concepts"** (Glassdoor, 2024). Confirmed topics include designing an inverted index/search engine and designing a billing system.
- 

Some variations report **3 coding rounds and 2 system design rounds**, plus a hiring manager round.



---

#### Onsite — Hiring Manager (HM) Round
- **Duration**: ~45–60 minutes
- 

There is a behavioral interview component with the hiring manager or team leads, focusing on past experiences, teamwork, and how you handle challenges, aimed at gauging cultural fit and alignment with Moveworks' values.


- **Watch out**: 

Some candidates report the HM round is where the process can go sideways — one reviewer described the HM as "hell bent on dissuading" them, taking responses out of context.



---

#### Onsite — Product Demo Round
- **Duration**: ~30 minutes
- 

Interview rounds include a product demo round, described as "very helpful to get a really good understanding of the product."


- 

This round may involve a walkthrough of Moveworks' product or a discussion with the hiring manager to evaluate the candidate's fit within the team and their understanding of the product.


- This round appears to be unique to Moveworks — candidates should come in with solid product knowledge.

---

#### Onsite — Leadership / CTO Round (sometimes included)
- 

Some candidates report a **chat with the CTO** as part of the virtual onsite alongside the hiring manager, two coding, and two system design rounds.


- 

A final interview with senior leadership is often more focused on behavioral questions, long-term vision, and alignment with company goals, as well as how past experiences relate to challenges at Moveworks.

### Red Flags & Insider Notes

- **Process is long and taxing.** 

Candidates report the process can be lengthy and demanding, sometimes lasting several hours. Be mentally prepared for this and ensure you have the stamina to engage fully throughout the day.


- **Recruiter communication is a consistent pain point.** 

Multiple candidates described a disappointing experience with the recruiting and coordination team, even while noting the technical portion was rigorous and well-structured.


- **Interviewers can be low-engagement.** 

Candidates describe an "extremely long process," with engineers who are "extremely disengaged" and give "zero positive or negative feedback" during coding problems.


- **Interviews are backend-heavy even for non-backend roles.** 

Even for frontend roles, the interviews are backend-heavy — brush up on backend skills, system design, and algorithmic challenges to cover all bases.


- **Product knowledge is rewarded.** 

Familiarity with Moveworks' current products and applications is consistently advised — show knowledge of their existing tools and the innovative solutions in the pipeline.


- **Down-leveling has been reported.** Multiple Blind threads mention candidates being down-leveled from their target Senior level after the loop.
