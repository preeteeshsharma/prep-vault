### Round Format & Platform



Fivetran officially describes two coding challenges. The first ("C1 – Problem Solving") is "a fairly standard coding question, designed to determine a base level of problem-solving ability in the software language of your choice," administered via HackerRank.





The interview process typically consists of two rounds, with candidates proceeding to Round 2 only after clearing Round 1. The first round of the technical interview lasts an hour and is divided into two parts, both focused on data structures and algorithms.





Round 2 is also a 45–60-minute coding interview on HackerRank, with ~30 minutes for the coding problem and ~15 minutes for discussion on other topics.

For SDE-2/Senior level, the interview structure typically includes 2 Coding rounds, 1 System Design round, and 1 Managerial round. "Coding round was based on Data Structure and Problem Solving (LC Medium to LC Hard)."

### Preparation Tips (from Candidates)

- 

Candidates advise: "Ensure you are well-versed in Java, SQL, and data structures. Practice coding problems similar to those found on LeetCode, particularly focusing on medium-level questions. Be prepared to explain your thought process and approach during live coding sessions."



- 

One candidate noted they expected a follow-up to optimize the LIS solution "to O(n log n) using binary search and patience sorting" — so be ready to discuss complexity improvements even if not explicitly asked.



- 

Multiple candidates flagged that **edge cases are critical**: Fivetran interviewers will not necessarily stop you if you miss an edge case — they let you code it out and discover it in testing, by which point it's too late. Enumerate edge cases explicitly before coding.



- 

One candidate mentioned on Blind that Fivetran's problems can reach "LC Hard" difficulty, so don't under-prepare by practicing only mediums.



- 

The process emphasizes "strong foundational knowledge in DSA" and "the ability to explain and justify your approach clearly."



- Java is heavily featured: 

Fivetran's second coding challenge (C2) "is given in the Java programming language, although there are very few advanced aspects of the language included, and the interviewer is instructed to assist with any minor language difficulties."
