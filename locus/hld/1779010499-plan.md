## Day 1 — System Design: High-Concurrency Transactional Systems

### Sysdesign: Design a Flash Sale System
- **Pattern / goal:** Build a distributed, high-concurrency inventory management system that prevents overselling, handles traffic spikes, and ensures transactional consistency under extreme load.
- **Approach:**
  - **Requirements phase (~5 min):** Clarify scope — single product vs. multi-product sale, geographic constraints, flash duration, concurrent buyer capacity, payment integration. Confirm in/out of scope: inventory decrement atomicity (IN), fraud detection (OUT), customer notification (OUT).
  - **Capacity estimates (~5 min):** Assume 100K concurrent buyers, 1K inventory units, 50 QPS peak during flash window. Work through latency targets (p99 < 500ms for inventory check + lock).
  - **API design (~8 min):** Define `/reserve-slot` (acquire distributed lock + reserve inventory), `/confirm-purchase` (charge payment + finalize), `/check-status` (poll reservation state). State idempotency keys for all mutations.
  - **Data model (~5 min):** Inventory record (product_id, available_count, version for optimistic locking), Reservation (buyer_id, product_id, status: PENDING/CONFIRMED/EXPIRED, ttl), Payment ledger (txn_id, idempotency_key, status).
  - **Core components (~15 min):** 
    - **Distributed lock layer** — Redis Redlock or Zookeeper for inventory mutations; explain why compare-and-swap alone fails under contention.
    - **Queue-based order processing** — Kafka topic for reservation events; async confirmation worker pool to decouple inventory lock from payment.
    - **Cache-aside pattern** — Redis for hot inventory counts with cache invalidation on every successful reservation; TTL-based refresh.
    - **Rate limiter** — Token bucket (Redis or in-memory) per user + IP to throttle buyers and prevent bot storms.
  - **Deep dive: inventory oversell prevention (~12 min):** Walk through a race condition scenario: two buyers attempt to buy the last unit simultaneously. Show how optimistic locking (version field) detects conflict; how Redlock acquires exclusive access; how the queue-based model ensures exactly-once processing.
  - **Bottlenecks & trade-offs (~5 min):** Discuss consistency vs. availability — strong consistency needed for inventory (use Redlock), eventual consistency acceptable for payment confirmation status. Address cache stampede risk and mitigation (probabilistic early refresh). Mention database sharding strategy if inventory table grows (shard by product_id).
- **Time:** 60 min

## Interview Tips (from Locus research)
- **Be conversational:** The HLD round at Locus is iterative; expect the interviewer to probe your design and steer the discussion. Draw connections to real logistics/dispatch problems (Locus's core domain) — mention how inventory locking parallels fleet slot allocation.
- **Know your past projects:** Locus interviewers dig into your prior work and may compare your design to systems you've built. Be ready to name a scalable system you've shipped and explain how its patterns apply here.
- **Focus on distributed transactions:** The Flash Sale question directly tests your understanding of race conditions, idempotency, and exactly-once semantics. Depth on Redis Redlock, Kafka, and DB-level optimistic locking will differentiate SDE3-level answers.
